What winning requires

Prizes are two-tiered, and the tier is gated on performance. A team cannot win both.

"teams performing better than last year's methods will be eligible for 'overall' case prizes, otherwise the '2026' prizes."

So the objective is not "place well" -- it is beat the 2025 methods re-scored under the 2026 metric. That is a 3× difference in prize money at first place. Separately there is $500 for best methodology report, and all submissions are eligible for it regardless of how the algorithm performed.

The metric

The official evaluation container is public on GitHub. We do not have to infer the metric or reimplement it -- we can run the exact scorer locally, which eliminates an entire class of risk before it exists.

Primary: BERTScore-F1, roberta-large, rescaled with baseline, taking the max over the five references, then meaned across cases. 

bert_scorer = BERTScorer(model_type="roberta-large", lang="en", rescale_with_baseline=True)
cands_expanded = [cand] * len(refs)
_P, _R, F1 = bert_scorer.score(cands_expanded, refs)
bertscore_f1s.append(F1.max().item())

Secondary metrics, all written to metrics.json but not ranked: NLI entailment (cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base), NLI × BERT, BLEU-4, ROUGE-1/2/L.

iTerse answers are optimal

Reference lists lead with the bare form -- "Yes", "No", "Cadiere Forceps", "Uterine horn" -- and scoring takes the max across references. An identical string scores F1 = 1.0, and rescaling maps 1.0 to 1.0. So emitting exactly "Yes" hits the ceiling. No elaborate answer can beat a one-word exact match, and a longer answer risks scoring lower.

iiCorrectness now dominates phrasing

Under n-gram overlap, a wrong-polarity answer that echoes the question's noun phrase still collects most of the credit. Under BERTScore against a terse reference, "No" scored against "Yes" has almost no lexical or semantic overlap to fall back on. The exact cost is unmeasured -- it is experiment #1 below -- but the direction is not in doubt.

Why this is good news

The routing and perception work is worth more under this metric, not less. A system that reasons its way to the right slot value is rewarded; one that merely sounds fluent is not.

iiiThe primary metric sees raw text

In evaluate.py, BLEU and ROUGE are computed on lowercased, punctuation-stripped strings -- but the candidate and references reach BERTScore and NLI un-normalized. Casing and terminal punctuation therefore affect the score that ranks us. The organizers' own test fixture probes exactly this: answer001c.json is "yes" against a ground truth of "Yes". We match reference casing exactly.

On not gaming it

NLI entailment genuinely captures contradiction, and it is reported alongside BERTScore in every result row. A hedging strategy that games semantic similarity would show up as a collapsed NLI score in the challenge paper. We optimize for being right.

What the task actually is

Analysis of the public sample set and the training label tables shows that Category 2 is not open-ended reasoning over surgical video. Three pieces of evidence point the same direction.

iThe container is invoked once per case

One video, one question, one answer. The time budget is therefore per case, not per test set -- which is what makes a multi-pass routing system affordable at all. 

/input/endoscopic-robotic-surgery-video.mp4
/input/visual-context-question.json    →  "Was a large needle driver used in this clip?"
/output/visual-context-response.json   →  "Yes"

iiEvery question is one slot

Five reference answers ship with each question, and scoring takes the best match. For the question above: 

"Yes"                                            ← the whole game
"Yes, a large needle driver was utilized."
"Yes, it appears a large needle driver was used."
"Yes, the procedure involved a large needle driver."
"Yes, needle driver is part of the procedure."

Perception has to supply exactly one thing: the polarity. Of the 11 sample questions, 7 are yes/no and the remaining 4 fill a single slot -- tool type, organ, procedure type, and tool purpose.

Provenance -- read before trusting the above

There is no 2026 sample set. The 2026 page links SURGVU25_cat_2_sample_set_public.zip as "10 video samples… to be used as reference" -- last year's set, last uploaded 2025-07-22, and the bucket holds no 2026 object at all. (It contains 11 cases, not 10.) Everything about question distribution is extrapolated from prior-year material, in a year where the metric changed and the label scope was restated. Where the 2026 site's text conflicts with these samples, the site wins.

The only 2026-native question evidence that exists publicly is inside the evaluation container. Its ground-truth fixtures are deliberately scrubbed -- case002 reads "No, the specific tool was not used", with the tool name redacted -- but test_metrics.py names three real questions, and its tool-question ground truth is "Forceps", class-level with no commercial name: 

Q1 (case001): In this procedure, what is cauterized by the surgeon?
Q5 (case005): Which surgical specialty is this procedure associated with?
Q6 (case006): Are forceps involved in the procedure?

Two of those are slot types the 11 prior-year samples do not cover at all -- surgical specialty, and anatomy-plus-action (what is cauterized, not merely whether cautery occurs). The taxonomy has been widened accordingly.

iiiGround truth was written from tables, not pixels

The tells are in the reference wording: "No, forceps are not mentioned", "Is a large needle driver among the listed tools?", "What procedure is this summary describing?" Those phrasings describe reading a spreadsheet, not watching a video. The spreadsheets in question:

TableColumns that matterWhat it supplies
tools.csv install_case_time, uninstall_case_time, arm, commercial_toolname, groundtruth_toolname The "listed tools" -- every row whose install interval covers the clip
tasks.csv start_time, stop_time, groundtruth_taskname, matched_description The "summary" -- the description of the covering task segment
Why this matters

If ground truth is generated from tables we already hold, then we can replicate the generator -- which turns an 11-example tuning set into thousands. Every decision below follows from this.

Consequence 1 -- the descriptions are a closed corpus of 21 strings

Across all 155 cases there are exactly 21 unique matched_description values, and most task classes map to one or two:

Task classUnique descriptions
Uterine horn1
Suspensory ligaments1
Range of motion1
Rectal artery / vein2
Retraction and collision avoidance2
Suturing12

The defending winner saw this same number and read it as a defect -- their report calls 21 captions "insufficient for training", and abandoned fine-tuning on that basis. That conclusion is correct and the inference from it is backwards. It is not a training set. It is a retrieval corpus. Classify the task, retrieve the string, and you are holding verbatim the text the ground-truth answers were generated from.

Twenty-one strings fit trivially in a YAML file baked into the container -- which also happens to satisfy the no-network constraint for free.

Consequence 2 -- exactly three instruments, out of twelve

The data description is explicit: for the extent of each clip "there are three robotic surgical tools installed and within the surgical field", and each clip "can contain up to three of 12 possible tools." That reconciles exactly with the arm structure in tools.csv -- four USM arms, one carrying the endoscope (nan(camera in)), leaving three instrument arms.

Build it in, don't learn it

The tool recogniser is a constrained top-3 selection over 12 classes, not twelve independent binary decisions. That collapses the hypothesis space from 212 to C(12,3) = 220, and makes the "predicted the wrong number of tools" failure mode impossible by construction.

Class scope is 12, not 20. The label tables do contain rarer classes -- suction irrigator, synchroseal, curved scissors, potts scissors, tenaculum forceps, bipolar dissector, crocodile grasper -- but the data description states that "these tools will not be part of the testing set and the submissions will not need to recognize those." They are auxiliary training signal and nothing more. The 2025 winner's 12-class list was correct.

Open question -- settle by measurement

Three of the eleven sample questions name a "large needle driver", which is a commercial_toolname. But the data description states that "the test labels will only be based on groundtruth_toolname column", unscoped to either category. Both cannot be fully true of the 2026 test set. Experiment 2 resolves it: if answering from the class alone matches ground truth as often as consulting P(commercial | class, task) priors, the priors are dead weight and the design drops a whole component.

Consequence 3 -- installed is not the same as visible

Ground truth keys off install intervals, and the challenge's own data description warns that tools may be "installed yet obscured from view." A needle driver mounted on USM1 but off-screen is still a yes.

So the estimation target is installation state -- which is temporally smooth, strongly predicted by task context, and a materially easier problem than detection. It also means the obvious instinct is a trap: getting better at seeing tools moves you away from the ground truth, not toward it.

Architecture

Two streams converge. The video stream recovers the metadata; the question stream determines which slot needs filling and at what granularity. They meet at the router, which decides whether the first VLM pass can be trusted or whether more evidence is worth buying.

The seven components

1Knowledge bundle (YAML)

Declarative, MONAI-bundle style, baked into the image. Four files: taxonomy.yaml (the 12 test-relevant classes, the 7 rare train-only classes, 43 commercial names mapped to class), descriptions.yaml (the 21 strings, keyed by task), answer_forms.yaml (surface form per slot type), and routing.yaml (thresholds, budgets, frame counts). Everything tunable lives here, so a sweep is a config sweep rather than a code change -- and it is also what makes the method legible in the report.

2Question parser

Text only, deterministic, no model. Emits a slot type (tool_presence, tool_type, organ, action, anatomy_action, procedure, specialty, world_knowledge), the queried entity normalized against the taxonomy, and the granularity. Questions like "What is the purpose of using forceps?" need no perception at all; specialty is answerable from the task class alone via YAML. The taxonomy is deliberately open, with a generic fallback path -- an unrecognized slot type must degrade, not fail, because the 2026 question distribution is not fully known.

3Tool install-state recogniser

Constrained top-3 selection over the 12 test-relevant classes, predicting installed rather than visible, with temporal smoothing across the clip. The exactly-three constraint is enforced at decode time rather than hoped for. Trained on SurgVU24 video against install intervals derived from tools.csv, optionally pre-trained on SurgToolLoc-2022 -- which the rules explicitly permit. The seven out-of-scope rare classes are kept as auxiliary training signal but never emitted.

4Task classifier and description retrieval

Classifies into 8 task classes (after normalizing the 15 raw label spellings), then retrieves the matching description. Organ is derived from the retrieved description via YAML, not from a separate classifier -- the description already names the anatomy, so a dedicated organ model is redundant. This replaces the 2025 winner's expensive 2048-token description-generation pass with a lookup that is both faster and closer to ground truth.

5VLM pass 1 with self-consistency

The VLM answers given the retrieved description, the tool list, and frames -- sampled N times over independent frame subsets. Confidence is the agreement rate across those samples: not a logprob, not a verbalized score. The budget comes from an inefficiency in the 2025 winner, who allocates 2048 output tokens to an answer that is one short sentence -- and under this metric the target is usually a single word.

6Router / arbiter

Plain Python and a threshold file -- no model. It fires when the VLM's slot value disagrees with the perception bank, or when self-consistency is low. The governing principle is narrow: escalate only to something that can actually reduce the uncertainty you have. If no evidence you could buy would change the answer, buy none.

SituationBranchSecond pass?Why
Agreement, high consistencyEmitnoNothing worth buying
Tool question, class-level, disagreement or flip-floppingDetector arbiteryesOcclusion is the classifier's known blind spot, and vision can fix it

The second pass runs only when new visual evidence arrived. The detector and dense re-sampling both produce something the model has not seen, so re-asking is worth the latency. The class fallback and the task-class-wins branch resolve by consulting a table -- re-asking there would add cost and give the model a chance to talk itself out of an answer that is already correct. Those two emit directly.

The detector never contributes location to the answer text -- only evidence that a tool is present.

7Answer-form selector

Chooses the surface form by slot type, then emits. Deliberately not left to the VLM -- the form choice is deterministic and sweepable, and we measure each option against the official scorer rather than arguing about it.

Casing and terminal punctuation are matched to the observed reference style, because the primary metric sees raw text.

Validation

Two pillars.

  • The official evaluation container, run locally. It is public, so our local number is the real number rather than an approximation. This replaces what would otherwise have been a metric reimplementation plus a calibration exercise.
  • A synthetic validation set. The 2025 winner tuned on 11 sample videos and had to hand-correct wrong answers inside them; 11 cases cannot support fitting a confidence threshold. Because ground truth is generated from metadata we already hold, we replicate the generator: sample a clip, read the covering tools.csv rows and tasks.csv segment, and emit a question plus five paraphrased references in the observed style -- terse first, then paraphrases.

The 11 public samples are held out from all tuning as a golden set, to detect drift between the synthetic distribution and the real one.

0Experiment 0 -- establish the bar

Run Capybara's public 2025 container against the official 2026 evaluation container on the 11-case sample set. This produces the BERTScore number we must beat for the "overall" prize tier, and gives us the strongest available baseline for every ablation. Repeat for the other three public prior-winner containers if time allows.

1Experiment 1 -- measure the metric's shape

Measure the metric's actual shape against the official scorer. Four numbers, all cheap to obtain, and every downstream decision about answer form depends on them:

  • What does a wrong-polarity answer cost? ("No" against a "Yes" reference set)
  • What does "yes" cost versus "Yes"? The organizers' own fixture asks this.
  • Does a full sentence ever beat the bare form when both are correct?
  • How much does a correct answer in the wrong register lose?

2Experiment 2 -- does commercial-name granularity matter?

For each of the three commercial-name sample questions, score answering from the class alone against answering from P(commercial | class, task) priors. If the class alone matches ground truth as often, the priors are dead weight and the design drops a component. A measurement, not an argument.

Error handling

Every failure degrades to a plausible answer rather than an exception. An empty output scores zero; a wrong-but-well-formed answer often scores above zero on the templated portion alone.

FailureResponse
Time budget exceededEmit the pass-1 answer, skip arbitration
Out of memoryReduce frame count, retry once, then classifier + template with no VLM
Parser finds no known slot typeGeneric VLM path with the retrieved description
Both streams low-confidenceAnswer from priors alone, via template
Any unhandled exceptionHighest-prior templated answer for the parsed slot type

Testing

Test-first, per standing practice on this project.

  • Unit -- question parser against every sample question plus adversarial phrasings; answer-form selector; prior computation from tools.csv.
  • Golden -- the 11 public samples, end to end, scored by the official container.
  • Integration -- the full container on a T4-class GPU. Not optional: the 2025 winner reports that 4-bit quantized VLMs produce different outputs on Turing versus Ampere versus Hopper, so validating on an A100 and submitting to a T4 means submitting untested code.
  • Ablation -- each component removed in turn on synthetic validation, to establish that the router earns its runtime.

Key decisions

  • Evidence arbitration over a literal confidence cascade or a full Bayesian slot filler. The cascade has no principled confidence signal; the Bayesian version needs calibration data for every evidence source and degrades badly if the reliabilities are wrong. This design keeps the cascade as its degenerate fallback and the Bayesian criterion as a one-function substitution in the router.
  • The second VLM pass is conditional on new visual evidence, not on escalation. Branches that resolve from a table emit directly. Re-asking the model about information it already had costs latency and risks it reasoning itself away from a correct answer.
  • Detector as evidence, not localization. No sample question asks where.
  • Retrieve the description, don't generate it. Twenty-one strings, closed set.
  • Answer tersely by default. An exact match with the terse reference is the ceiling; elaboration can only cost.
  • Optimize for correctness, not for gaming the metric. NLI entailment is reported alongside BERTScore and would expose any hedging strategy.
  • Target installation state, not visibility. That is what the ground truth encodes.
  • No separate organ classifier. Redundant with description retrieval.
  • Fully independent of OpScribe. No shared code, no 72B model, no fine-tuned adapters. The T4's 16 GB settles this regardless of preference.

Risks

Largest by a wide margin

The question-distribution evidence is a year stale. There is no 2026 sample set; the 11 cases we tune against are 2025 reference material, in a year where the metric changed from BLEU to BERTScore and the tool-label scope was restated as class-level. The three 2026-native questions visible in the evaluation container already reveal two slot types the 11 do not contain. It is entirely possible the 2026 question generator was rewritten.

Mitigations: hold the 11 out of all tuning; keep the slot taxonomy open with a graceful generic fallback; treat any large synthetic-versus-golden gap as stop-and-reassess rather than something to tune away; and spend one preliminary submission early and deliberately, to sample the real distribution before committing the architecture's tunables.

Open -- unpublished

The per-phase runtime limit is unknown. It bounds how many passes the router can afford. Requested from the organizers; until it is known, routing.yaml carries a conservative budget and the design degrades gracefully under a tighter one.

Open -- data

2026 labels may differ from the SurgVU25 labels in the bucket. Since ground truth is generated from those exact strings, stale labels would corrupt every measurement silently. To be checked against the challenge portal's official download.

Deadline -- 10 days out

Registration closes Aug 15. The team must exist on the platform and the signed agreement form must have reached isi.challenges@intusurg.com. Acceptance was confirmed 2026-06-11, but platform-side team creation should be verified rather than assumed.

Managed

Quantization is nondeterministic across GPU architectures, per the 2025 winner's own report -- mitigated by the T4 integration-test requirement above. Separately, both submission budgets are finite: 10 preliminary attempts, then 2 final ones. Every submission must be justified by a local result first.

Out of scope

  • Category 1 tool localization. A detector is trained here only as an internal arbiter; beating the Cat 1 prize bar is not a goal.
  • Any use of challenge test data for training or tuning.
  • Fine-tuning the VLM. The 2025 winner tried it, it failed for a structural reason, and this design routes around the need.
